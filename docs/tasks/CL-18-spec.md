# CL-18 — Redesign Cash Forecast (/forecast) with a liquidity ruler and recurring items

## Scope
Redesign of the Cash Flow Forecast screen (`/forecast`), replacing the current raw layout with a light-themed KPI panel, a chronological liquidity ruler and a modern management list for recurring items (fixed income and expenses). The screen highlights the projected balance on the day before the next main salary, identifies the lowest liquidity point in the next 30 days, and allows creating, editing and re-syncing recurring items.

**Two distinct time windows, deliberately not unified.** The KPI **`Mínimo Projetado (30d)`** reads the next **30 days** — it is the short-term risk gauge and its label keeps saying `30d`. The **liquidity ruler** renders the next **90 days**. These windows are independent on purpose: widening the KPI to 90 days would dilute the short-term risk signal it exists to give, and narrowing the ruler back to 30 days would remove the forward visibility the ruler exists to give. An implementer must not "unify" them.

Covered:
- Three projection KPI cards at the top, laid out as `grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-3` (the fourth card is removed by this revision, so the row must be re-laid-out to three equal columns — it must not keep a 4-column grid with an empty cell, nor leave one card dangling alone on a second line at any breakpoint):
  - **Saldo Pré-Salário**: projected bank balance on the day immediately before the main salary is received (`Forecast.balance_on(projection, Date.add(next_income_date, -1))`).
  - **Mínimo Projetado (30d)**: lowest `balance_after` among the occurrences of the next 30 days, with the date it occurs and a status badge. The badge reads **`Seguro`** when that minimum is `>= 0` and **`Em risco`** when it is `< 0`; there is no third state and no other threshold.
  - **Projeção em 12 Meses**: the **projected final balance at the end of the 12-month horizon** — that is, `balance_after` of the last occurrence of `Forecast.project(365)`, or the starting balance when the projection has no occurrence. It is a balance, not a delta. Next to it a badge shows the percentage change of that projected balance against the current balance: `(projected_final - starting_balance) / starting_balance * 100`, rounded to a whole percent and signed. The badge is omitted when `starting_balance` is zero.
- **No count is displayed anywhere on this screen.** The `Recorrências Ativas` KPI card (item count plus consolidated income/expense totals) is **removed** and must not be reinstated in any form — not as a card, not as a badge, not in parentheses beside a heading. This is a standing product requirement, not a preference: the same removal has now been applied to the sibling screens of CL-16 and CL-17, and the rule for this screen is absolute — the rendered page contains no quantity-of-records indicator. The consolidated income/expense totals disappear with the card and are not relocated elsewhere.
- **Liquidity ruler (next 90 days)**: chronological cards for **every** element of `projection.occurrences` whose date falls inside the 90-day window — no truncation, no filtering by kind. This includes credit-card bill occurrences produced by `Forecast.card_occurrences/2` alongside the recurring ones. This is deliberate: the ruler is a *liquidity* view, a card boleto is one of the largest liquidity events a month has, and the running balance chain computed by `Forecast.project/1` already includes those events — filtering them out would leave every "Saldo após" figure unexplained by the amounts on screen. Card occurrences are therefore rendered with the same date / description / signed impact / "Saldo após" layout, but are **visually distinguished** by a card icon and a `Fatura de cartão` marker so a reader can tell them apart from recurring items. The occurrence with the lowest resulting balance **within the 90-day ruler window** is marked as the critical point; this marker is independent of the 30-day KPI and the two may point at different occurrences. Ordering is by settlement date; the section header carries no event count and reads `Régua Temporal de Liquidez (Próximos 90 Dias)`. The ruler is a **single horizontal row** that scrolls horizontally inside its own card — see "Ruler layout" below.
- **Installment disclosure on the card-bill row**: a card-bill occurrence whose amount includes projected installment parcels states how much of it they are (`dos quais R$ X são parcelas`), and makes the contributing installment groups reachable from that row for detail. See "Installment disclosure" below for the exact semantics and for why an installment never becomes a ruler event of its own.
- **Recurring items list**: one row per registered item with a day-of-month calendar badge, category, average amount, active/inactive toggle and main-salary marker. The section header is the plain label `Lançamentos Recorrentes Cadastrados` and **must not** display an item count in parentheses or anywhere else; the list is unfiltered, so every registered item is rendered as a row. Card bill occurrences are projection events, not registered items, and never appear in this list.
- Global **"Sincronizar Histórico"** action recalculating recurrences from the real transactions of recent months, now accompanied by **on-screen copy stating what the sync does and what it does not do** — see "Sync explanation copy" below.
- **Edit modal** for an existing recurring item: change average amount, usual settlement day and the main-salary flag, plus a "Ressincronizar com histórico" action restoring the history-derived value (`resync_item`).
- **Create action for a recurring item** (`Novo Item Recorrente`), next to the `Lançamentos Recorrentes Cadastrados` heading, opening the same modal in create mode. See "Creating a recurring item" below — this supersedes the earlier decision that the screen offered no create path.
- **Temporary recurrences**: commitments (`CashLens.Installments.InstallmentGroup`) whose parcels are *not* already inside a credit-card bill enter the ruler as recurring events with a known end date and a parcel position. **Honest sizing, verified against the operator's database:** it currently holds **73 installment groups, every one of them `credit_card` and every one of them linked to transactions on a credit-card account** — zero `financing`, zero `consorcio`. All 73 are therefore excluded by the membership rule below, because their parcels already ride the card-bill estimate. **Implementing this changes nothing on screen against today's data**; a reviewer must not expect a visible difference. It starts paying off the first time the operator records a financing or a consórcio paid from a current account — the one case that is *absent from the forecast entirely today*.
- Updated/added tests in `test/cash_lens_web/live/forecast_live_test.exs`.

Not covered:
- Deleting a recurring item, or editing the `category_id` of an existing one (the category is chosen once, at creation).
- Changes to the `CashLens.Forecast.RecurringItem` schema or new migrations. Creation uses the schema and the `create_recurring_item/1` function exactly as they exist today.
- Changes to the daily projection math in `CashLens.Forecast.project/0`, including how card occurrences are estimated.
- Any filtering, pagination or "show more" behaviour on the recurring items list.

## Approach
### 1. Behavior
- `/forecast` keeps being served by `CashLensWeb.ForecastLive.Index`.
- On mount, the LiveView runs `Forecast.project()` and derives the minimum-balance point, the pre-salary balance and the 12-month projected final balance to feed the KPI cards.
- The ruler takes every occurrence of `projection.occurrences` dated within the next **90 days**, in date order, and renders the running balance after each one — no kind-based filtering, and consequently no recomputation of the running balance: the `balance_after` value assigned by `Forecast.project/1` is rendered as-is.
- No second projection is run for the 90-day window. `Forecast.project/1` already takes a horizon in days and defaults to `@default_horizon_days = 365`; the screen already computes that 365-day projection for the `Projeção em 12 Meses` KPI. The ruler, the `Mínimo Projetado (30d)` KPI and the 12-month KPI are therefore three **date slices of the same single projection** — 90 days, 30 days and the full horizon respectively. The 30-day KPI keeps its 30-day slice and its `30d` label unchanged by this task.
- A ruler card has three variants, discriminated by which extra key the occurrence map carries. `:origin` (`:boleto` / `:estimado`), produced only by `Forecast.card_occurrences/2`, renders the **card-bill** variant with the card icon and the `Fatura de cartão` marker. `:commitment`, produced only by the temporary-recurrence builder, renders the **temporary-recurrence** variant (see below). Neither key present means a plain recurring occurrence carrying a `%RecurringItem{}` under `:item`. The three keys are mutually exclusive; everything else in the card layout is identical across variants.
- The card whose resulting balance is the minimum of the window gets the critical-point highlight, whether it is a recurring occurrence or a card bill.
- The recurring items list renders every item returned by the context, active and inactive alike. No count is rendered next to the heading.
- Toggling active/inactive and the salary flag triggers `toggle_active` / `toggle_salary` and recomputes the projection immediately.
- Editing opens a LiveView modal pre-filled with the item's current attributes; saving persists and recomputes; "Ressincronizar com histórico" restores the history-derived amount and day.

#### Ruler layout
- The ruler renders as **one single row**, never wrapping: the current `grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-4` container is replaced by a horizontally scrollable flex strip (`flex` + `overflow-x-auto`) whose children are fixed-width, non-shrinking event cards (narrower than today's grid cells, so more events are visible at once).
- **Card content order is fixed** — top to bottom, every ruler card, both variants:
  1. the date;
  2. the reason (the item label, or `Fatura <conta>` for a card bill; the `Fatura de cartão` marker and the card icon stay attached to this line because they identify *what* the event is);
  3. the event's signed amount (and, on an `:estimado` card bill only, the `dos quais R$ X são parcelas` disclosure line with its expandable group detail, immediately below the amount it qualifies);
  4. the `Saldo após` label, on its own line;
  5. the running balance figure.
  No content is added or dropped by this reordering — only the arrangement changes.
- **The running balance is the visually dominant figure of the card.** The ruler exists to answer "how much is left after this happens", not "how big was this event": the `<saldo>` is therefore rendered larger and heavier than the event's own amount (in the mock, `text-lg font-black` for the balance against `text-xs font-bold` for the amount), inverting today's emphasis, where the amount is the prominent number. An implementer must not restore the amount to equal or greater visual weight.
- The horizontal scroll must live **inside the ruler card only**. The page itself must not gain a horizontal scrollbar: the strip's container is width-constrained and clips its overflow, and the strip is reachable by trackpad/wheel scroll with a visible scrollbar (do not hide it with `scrollbar-hide`).

#### Creating a recurring item
The screen gains a `Novo Item Recorrente` action beside the `Lançamentos Recorrentes Cadastrados` heading. It calls the already-public `CashLens.Forecast.create_recurring_item/1` (`lib/cash_lens/forecast.ex:28`). Facts this behavior rests on, verified against the code:

- **One recurring item per category, enforced by the database.** `priv/repo/migrations/20260702000000_consolidated_schema.exs:206` declares `create unique_index(:recurring_items, [:category_id])`, and `RecurringItem.changeset/2` both `validate_required`s `:category_id` and carries a matching `unique_constraint(:category_id)`. Offering a category that already has an item can therefore only end in a constraint error on save.
- **`sync_all/0` overwrites items it does not consider manually edited.** For a fixed category that already has an item, `sync_one/3` calls `suggest_for_category` and writes the history-derived `day_of_month` and `amount` over whatever is stored (`forecast.ex:110-119`). The single clause that protects an item is `sync_one(_category, %RecurringItem{manually_edited: true}, acc), do: acc` (`forecast.ex:93`).

Behavior:
- **Candidate categories.** The category selector offers exactly the categories that have **no** recurring item yet, minus credit-card categories. A new public `Forecast.list_categories_without_recurring_item/0` builds that list: `Categories.list_categories()`, rejecting the ones whose id appears in `list_recurring_items()` and the ones matched by the existing `credit_card_category?/1` predicate (which the new function reuses in-module), ordered by name. Credit-card categories are excluded because card bills already enter the projection through `Forecast.card_occurrences/2`; registering one as a recurring item would count the same money twice. The list is deliberately **not** restricted to `type == "fixed"`: a hand-created item exists precisely for a recurrence the history sync cannot detect, and `sync_all/0` only ever visits fixed categories anyway.
- **Empty candidate list.** When every eligible category already has a recurring item, the create action is still rendered but **disabled**, and opening is impossible; the modal is never shown with an empty select. The disabled control carries the explanation `Todas as categorias já possuem um lançamento recorrente`.
- **The created item is persisted with `manually_edited: true`.** This is the critical requirement. Without it, an operator types a value by hand and the very next press of this screen's own `Sincronizar Histórico` button silently replaces their `day_of_month` and `amount` with the median of transaction history — the item would be created only to be overwritten from the same screen. `Forecast.manual_update/2` already forces the same flag on edit (`forecast.ex:149-153`); creation must match it. The LiveView therefore merges `"manually_edited" => true` into the attrs it passes to `create_recurring_item/1` (it is already in the changeset's `cast` list).
- **Intended consequence, stated explicitly:** a hand-created item stays outside the history sync permanently — `sync_all/0` will never touch it — until someone opens it and uses the modal's existing "Ressincronizar com histórico" action, whose `Forecast.resync_item/1` clears the flag (`forecast.ex:137`). That is correct and deliberate: the operator chose the number, not the history.
- **Form fields**, matching the real changeset: `category_id` (required, select from the candidate list), `label` (required, text, pre-filled with the chosen category's name and editable), `day_of_month` (required integer, validated 1..31 by the changeset and additionally constrained by `min`/`max` on the input) and `amount` (required decimal, rejected when zero by the changeset's `validate_amount_not_zero/1`, with the sign carrying the direction — negative for an expense, positive for income).
- **`active` and `is_salary` are not exposed at creation.** `active` takes its schema default `true`. `is_salary` takes its default `false` and is **deliberately absent from the create form**: marking the salary is a *global* operation — `Forecast.set_as_salary/1` clears `is_salary` on every other row first (`forecast.ex:161-164`) — whereas `create_recurring_item/1` would simply insert a second `is_salary: true` row without clearing the others, leaving `next_income_date/1` silently picking whichever occurrence comes first. The salary marker is set after creation, from the list row's existing salary toggle, which routes through `set_as_salary/1`.
- **Creation reuses the existing edit modal**, in a `:new` mode, rather than getting its own. The two forms share `label`, `day_of_month` and `amount`, and duplicating that markup would guarantee the two drift apart. The mode drives exactly three differences: the title, the category select (create only — the category of an existing item is not editable), and the "Ressincronizar com histórico" action (edit only — it is meaningless before there is history-derived state to restore). A `save_item` submit with no `edit_item` assigned routes to `create_recurring_item/1`; with one, to `manual_update/2`, unchanged.
- On success the modal closes and the projection is recomputed through the existing `assign_projection/1`, so the new item appears in the list and its occurrences appear in the ruler without a page reload. On a changeset error the modal stays open and renders the message.

#### Sync explanation copy
The `Sincronizar Histórico` action gains a short, always-visible explanation rendered next to it, so the screen answers by itself what the sync covers and what it silently leaves alone. It is presentational: no behavior of `Forecast.sync_all/0` changes. Facts it must state, each verified against the code:

- **Manual only.** `lib/cash_lens_web/live/forecast_live/index.ex:69-70` (`handle_event("sync_all", …)`) is the sole caller of `Forecast.sync_all/0` in the whole repository; `lib/cash_lens/application.ex` starts Oban but registers no cron plugin and the single existing worker (`CashLens.Workers.RecalculateBalanceWorker`) does not call it. Nothing runs the sync on a schedule.
- **Scope of the walk.** `sync_all/0` (`forecast.ex:81-91`) visits only categories with `type == "fixed"` that are not credit-card categories, the latter matched by `credit_card_category?/1` (slug `cartao-de-credito` or a `cartao-de-credito-` prefix).
- **History requirement.** `suggest_for_category/1` (`forecast.ex:39-60`) reads that category's transactions of the last `@history_months = 6` months **in non-credit-card accounts only** (`a.is_credit_card == false`) and returns `:insufficient_history` below `@min_occurrences = 2`, in which case nothing is created or updated.
- **Manually edited items are never touched.** The clause `sync_one(_category, %RecurringItem{manually_edited: true}, acc), do: acc` (`forecast.ex:93`) skips them outright; a non-edited item is re-derived and overwritten (`forecast.ex:110-119`).
- **Day and amount come from different places.** `day_of_month` is the **median** of the transaction days, while `amount` is the amount of the **most recent** transaction — not a median or an average (`forecast.ex:56-58`). The copy states this asymmetry, because it is what explains an atypical last month becoming the forecast figure.
- **No automatic detection.** Nothing scans variable or uncategorized categories looking for periodicity; a category becomes eligible only once the operator marks it Fixa.

The copy is written in Portuguese, sits beside the action without dominating the card, and is rendered unconditionally (not behind a tooltip, popover or disclosure toggle), so it is assertable in a LiveView render. Exact text:

> **Sincronização manual — só acontece quando você clica aqui.**
> Percorre apenas categorias marcadas como **Fixo (Contas Essenciais)** — cartões de crédito ficam de fora — e precisa de pelo menos **2 lançamentos nos últimos 180 dias** em contas que não sejam cartão. O dia vem da **mediana** dos lançamentos; o valor vem do **lançamento mais recente**. Esta sincronização não altera itens editados à mão; para atualizar um deles, use **Ressincronizar com Histórico** no próprio item. Nenhuma recorrência é descoberta sozinha: marcar a categoria como Fixo é o que a torna elegível.

Three precision points, each corrected after review:

- **180 dias, not "6 meses".** The window is `Date.add(Date.utc_today(), -30 * @history_months)` — 180 days, which is not six calendar months. The copy states what the code does.
- **`median/1` returns the lower middle value on an even-sized list**, so with exactly two transactions the *earlier* day wins. It is not an average, and an implementer must not "correct" it to one.
- **The no-touch claim is scoped to this sync.** `Forecast.resync_item/1`, reached from the per-item `Ressincronizar com Histórico` action, deliberately does override a `manually_edited` item and clears the flag. An absolute "never altered" would have been false.
- **The label matches `/categories`.** CL-16 ships that flag as `Fixo (Contas Essenciais)`; copy telling the operator to go and mark a category is only actionable if it names the control they will actually see.

#### Installment disclosure
Facts this behavior rests on, verified against the code:
- The recurring items on this screen come from **categories with `type == "fixed"`** (`Forecast.sync_all/0`, filtered at `lib/cash_lens/forecast.ex:84`), derived from transaction history. They are **not** derived from installment groups. A row such as the mock's `Plano de Saúde Familiar` is such a fixed category.
- Installment groups (`CashLens.Installments.InstallmentGroup`) already affect the projection, but **only inside the credit-card bill estimate**. `Forecast.estimate_for_month/2` (`forecast.ex:355-377`) takes the most recent statement, computes `variable = statement_amount(statement) + Installments.account_installment_total(account_id, recent_month)` and then returns `Decimal.sub(variable, future_installments)` where `future_installments = Installments.account_installment_total(account_id, month)`. The sign convention is why it adds then subtracts: `statement_amount/1` is **negative** (an outflow) while `account_installment_total/2` returns a **positive magnitude**, so adding the reference month's installments strips them out of the statement and subtracting the projected month's installments adds those back as an outflow.
- The calculation **does not change** in this task. The disclosure is presentational only.

Behavior:
- `Forecast.build_card_occurrence/4` (currently arity 4) is a private helper and is the natural carrier for the disclosure: the occurrence map it builds — the map that `Forecast.card_occurrences/2` returns publicly — gains an installment field (the projected month's installment total, as a positive magnitude) and the list of contributing installment groups, so the number `estimate_for_month/2` already computes internally stops being discarded. Its arity/signature changes accordingly; the `%{date:, item:, balance_after:, origin:}` keys it already produces are kept, so `with_running_balance/2` — which reads only `occ.item.amount` — and every existing consumer stay untouched.
- **How the total reaches the occurrence:** do not widen `estimate_for_month/2`'s return value. `card_occurrence_for_date/2` (`forecast.ex:317-318`) already computes `month = Date.beginning_of_month(date)` and holds it in scope for both branches, so it calls the new `CashLens.Installments` function with `{account.id, month}` and passes the result into `build_card_occurrence`. This keeps `estimate_for_month/2`'s contract — a single `Decimal` — unchanged.
- **The disclosure month is `Date.beginning_of_month(due_date)`**, the same `month` that `card_occurrence_for_date/2` uses to look up the statement — *not* the statement's `competencia`. `competencia` appears only inside `estimate_for_month/2`, where it identifies the *reference* month whose installments are stripped out of the historical statement; it is not the projected month being disclosed. Conflating the two would disclose the wrong month's parcels.
- Exposing the contributing groups needs a **new public function in `CashLens.Installments`** that returns the groups behind `account_installment_total/2` for a given `{account_id, month}` — the same query plus `parcel_due_in_month?/2` filter that function already performs, returning the groups (with their parcel value) rather than only their sum. `account_installment_total/2` keeps its current signature and behavior.
- The ruler's card-bill row renders `dos quais R$ X são parcelas` when that total is greater than zero, and omits the line when it is zero. The contributing groups are reachable from the row (expandable detail listing description, parcel value and progress).
- **Only `:estimado` occurrences carry the disclosure.** A `:boleto` occurrence is a real imported statement: its amount is the statement's own total, `estimate_for_month/2` is never called for it, and this task computes no installment breakdown for it. `:boleto` rows render no disclosure line.
- **Guard against a future "improvement":** an installment must **not** be promoted to its own ruler event. Promoting each installment to a ruler row without reducing the card-bill estimate by exactly the same amount would count that money twice and corrupt every subsequent `Saldo após` in the chain, since `with_running_balance/2` sums every occurrence's amount in order. That is precisely why installments are disclosed *inside* the bill rather than listed beside it.

#### Temporary recurrences (off-card commitments)
A commitment is a recurrence with a known end: `InstallmentGroup` already stores `start_date`, `installments` (a count) and `total_amount`, so its parcel value and its last billing month (`Installments.last_installment_date/1` = `add_months(start_date, installments - 1)`) are both derivable. The parcel value is **`Installments.parcel_value/1` — `Decimal.div(total_amount, installments) |> Decimal.round(2)`** — the quotient **rounded to two decimal places**, not the raw division. This matters: for a plan whose total is not divisible by its parcel count the two differ (1000/3 is 333.3333… versus 333.33), which would give the ruler a different amount, a different `Saldo após` chain, and a different figure from the one the card-bill disclosure already shows for the very same group. Use the existing helper; do not re-derive the division. **No migration and no schema change are needed.**

**Membership rule — origin of the money, not the label.** A commitment is a temporary recurrence **iff none of its linked transactions sits on an account with `is_credit_card == true`**. `commitment_type` is *not* the discriminator. The reason is exactly the double count guarded against in "Installment disclosure": `Installments.account_installment_total/2` selects groups by `join: t in assoc(g, :transactions), where: t.account_id == ^account_id` (`installments.ex:29-39`), and `Forecast.card_occurrences/2` calls it only for accounts where `is_credit_card` is true. So the money of a parcel is already in the ruler **precisely when** that parcel's transactions live on a credit-card account — which is the same condition, stated positively. Deciding by `commitment_type` instead would mean that a financing mistyped as `credit_card`, or a card purchase mistyped as `financing`, silently changes the arithmetic: the latter would be counted twice (once inside the bill estimate, once as its own ruler event) and corrupt every later `Saldo após`. The chosen rule cannot do that, because the same fact drives both sides.

**The rule is a deliberate superset, and the gap is pre-existing.** `card_accounts/0` (`forecast.ex:297-303`) filters not merely `is_credit_card` but `is_credit_card and not is_closed and is_integer(closing_day) and is_integer(due_day)`. So a commitment on a **closed** card, or on a card with no closing/due day configured, is excluded from the ruler by this rule *and* absent from any bill estimate — invisible in the forecast. That is exactly its status today and this task does not change it; the asymmetry is recorded here so it is not mistaken for a regression. The direction of the superset is the safe one: bill-inclusion is a strict subset of ruler-exclusion, so the worst case is a commitment shown nowhere, never one counted twice.
- A group with **no linked transactions at all** (a commitment just registered, before any parcel was imported) satisfies the rule and is included. That is correct, not a loophole: contributing to no account's `account_installment_total/2`, it is in no bill, so counting it once on the ruler is exactly right. When its first parcel is later imported onto a card account, the group leaves the ruler in the same instant the bill estimate starts carrying it — there is no moment in which both hold it.
- `is_contemplated` / `contemplated_at` make **no difference**. Verified: they are written by the consórcio form (`installment_live/index.html.heex:534`) and read in exactly one place, a display line that prints `Contemplado` / `Aguardando contemplação` (`index.html.heex:386-392`). No projection, parcel-value or due-month function reads them. Contemplation means the quota has drawn its credit letter; the monthly parcels continue unchanged until the plan ends. A contemplated consórcio therefore projects exactly like a non-contemplated one, and this task must not branch on the flag.

**How the occurrence is built and where it joins.**
- A new public `CashLens.Installments.list_off_card_groups/0` returns the groups matching the membership rule, using a `NOT EXISTS` sub-query over transactions joined to accounts on `is_credit_card`. `account_installment_total/2` keeps its current signature and behavior.
- The parcel value and the parcel position must be readable from outside the context: `parcel_value/1` and `parcel_due_in_month?/2` are private today (`installments.ex:217-233`), so this task exposes what it needs — a public parcel value and a public "which parcel falls in this month" helper returning the 1-based index (`month_diff(month_start(start_date), month) + 1`) or `nil` outside the plan's window. `last_installment_date/1` is already public.
- A new `Forecast` builder emits, for each off-card group and each month between today and `horizon_end` in which a parcel falls, one occurrence dated on that month's parcel day — `start_date`'s day clamped to the month's length, through the same `clamp_day/3` the recurring items use. Occurrences dated before today are dropped, so a parcel whose day already passed in the current month does not appear, matching `next_occurrence_date/2`'s behavior for recurring items.
- The occurrence map keeps the shape every consumer expects: `%{date:, item: %{id:, label:, amount:, is_salary: false}, balance_after: nil}` plus `commitment: %{group_id:, parcel_number:, installments:, ends_on:, commitment_type:}`. `amount` is the **negated** parcel value (an outflow, the same sign convention as a card bill), `label` is the group's `description_pattern`, `is_salary` is `false`. `with_running_balance/2` reads only `occ.item.amount`, so it needs no change.
- `Forecast.project/1` concatenates the new list with `recurring_occurrences ++ card_occurrences(...)` before the existing `Enum.sort_by(& &1.date, Date)` and running-balance pass. **The card-bill maths is untouched**: `estimate_for_month/2`, `account_installment_total/2` and `build_card_occurrence/…` are not modified by this addition, and no group that feeds a bill can produce a temporary occurrence.

**The month it ends, and after it.** The last occurrence generated is the one for `add_months(start_month, installments - 1)`; it is rendered with its parcel position equal to the total (`48/48`) and an explicit *last parcel* marker. No occurrence is generated for any later month, so from the following month on the projection simply stops subtracting that amount and the running balance rises accordingly. Nothing needs to be deactivated or deleted for that to happen — the end is derived, and a plan that ended in the past produces no occurrence at all.

**Ruler only; not in the recurring-items list.** A temporary recurrence appears on the ruler and **not** in `Lançamentos Recorrentes Cadastrados`. That list is defined as one row per registered `RecurringItem`, with per-row controls (`toggle_active`, `set_as_salary`, edit, resync) that a commitment has none of, and it already excludes card bills for the same reason: a derived event is not a registered item. Commitments are managed on their own screen (`/installments`), and duplicating them here would offer edit controls that do not apply and imply they could be toggled off.

**`Forecast.sync_all/0` is untouched.** A temporary recurrence is derived from an `InstallmentGroup` at projection time and is never persisted: no `RecurringItem` row is created for a commitment, `sync_all/0` keeps walking only fixed categories from transaction history, and the `Sincronizar Histórico` copy settled above stays literally unchanged (it describes that walk, which still covers exactly what it says).

## Out of Scope
- Deleting recurring items, and editing the category of an existing one.
- Exposing the main-salary flag in the create form (it is global; set it afterwards from the list toggle).
- Database schema changes or new migrations.
- Changes to the core projection math, including card bill estimation: the installment disclosure is presentational and must leave every amount and every `Saldo após` identical.
- Promoting **card-backed** installments to their own ruler events (would double-count; see "Installment disclosure"). Only commitments outside any card bill become temporary recurrences.
- Creating, editing or deleting commitments from `/forecast` (they are managed on `/installments`), and any change to `InstallmentGroup`'s schema, to `commitment_type`'s allowed values or to `Forecast.sync_all/0`.
- Any installment breakdown for `:boleto` (real imported statement) card occurrences.
- Widening the `Mínimo Projetado (30d)` KPI beyond 30 days.
- Filtering/pagination of the recurring items list.
